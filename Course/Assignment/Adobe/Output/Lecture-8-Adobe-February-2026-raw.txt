………………………………………………………………………………………………………………………………………… Recall … What do technology shocks do? ● Technological innovations 1. Change what we consume by rendering some products and services obsolete 2. Change the way we do things by rendering some business processes obsolete ● They result in organizational change at the industry and company level, forcing customers and producers to adapt ● Adoption and adaptation are 1) costly, and these costs flow through the income statement as companies “redo” themselves 2) risky as there is always uncertainty as to whether the company will succeed in redoing itself ● As a result, financial markets veer between the gains (and excitement) of the new and the costs and risks of the adaptation to the new … 9Tano Santos Heilbrunn Center

===== PAGE 40 =====
………………………………………………………………………………………………………………………………………… ADBE: Licensing vs. subscription: The durable good problem – 2 ● Key insight: Selling versus renting for a monopolist – When selling a durable good, demand is reduced. – This leads to a price drop to attract the next consumer – But if early customer anticipate this, they can delay purchase and obtain a better price – The monopolist ends up charging the competitive price (the Coase conjecture) – The problem is that the monopolist cannot commit not to produce more; an additional unit produced imposes a cost on the a past customer: The monopolist does not internalize that cost • Art pieces versus cars – “Renting”: Instead in a subscription model the costs of a change in the subscription fee also affects past subscribers (who can always liquidate their subscription and get a new one!): The monopolist internalizes this and that acts as commitment to limit supply and maintain high prices – Renting or subscription is the way to go for software producers 40 T ano Santos Heilbrunn Center
